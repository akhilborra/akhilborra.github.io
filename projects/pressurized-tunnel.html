Max Planck Institute · 2025–present

Rotor performance at high Reynolds number

Model wind turbines in ordinary wind tunnels run at Reynolds numbers far below those of utility-scale rotors, so their performance doesn't transfer directly. A pressurized tunnel closes that gap. I'm measuring how power, thrust and optimal blade pitch change as the chord Reynolds number climbs into the millions.

Campaign

I planned and ran a campaign in the Variable Density Turbulence Tunnel at MPI-DS that measured the power and thrust coefficients of a model wind turbine across Reynolds number and blade pitch angle, and built the LabVIEW acquisition pipeline that ran it.

Optimal pitch

The pitch angle that maximizes power also shifts with Reynolds number. That matters when lab-scale results are used to tune control strategies for real turbines.

Ongoing work

  • Designing test stands to measure the efficiency of the rotor's internal drivetrain and calibrating the strain gauges used for thrust.
  • Designing a new rotor in CAD, scaled from the IEA 15 MW reference turbine, for fabrication and testing.
  • Benchmarking an OpenFAST blade element momentum model against the measurements to quantify its prediction errors and trace their physical sources.
