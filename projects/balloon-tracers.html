Balloons as Lagrangian tracers in wind farms

How efficiently a wind farm works depends on how it mixes with the atmospheric boundary layer, which replenishes the momentum the turbines extract. These mixing processes are Lagrangian by nature but are usually studied with fixed-point instruments. We built a system of neutrally buoyant balloons that drift with the flow and report back what they experience.

Site
WINSENT test site, Stuttgart (DE)
Platform
DJI Matrice 350 drone
Tracers
Balloons + radiosondes
Published
J. Phys.: Conf. Ser., 2026

Idea

Wind turbines sit in the atmospheric surface layer and act like a sudden jump in surface roughness. An internal boundary layer grows through the farm, and shear at its edge pulls high-momentum air down through turbulent mixing. Following individual parcels of air through that process lets us see the mixing and entrainment as the flow itself does.

Instead of fixed instruments, we let the air carry the sensors: neutrally buoyant balloons drift with the flow through and around the wind farm, recording what each parcel of air experiences along the way.

Experimental design

  • Tracers. I designed an integrated hardware and software payload that pairs neutrally buoyant balloons with radiosondes, which transmit position, pressure, temperature and humidity to a ground receiver.
  • Release. I designed the launch payload for a DJI Matrice 350 drone: a half-dome hub with four carbon-fibre rods hangs below the drone, and four balloons, each carrying its own radiosonde, are held at the rod tips by strings that run through the rods into the hub. At release height upstream of the turbines, heated wires inside the hub burn through all four strings at once, and the balloons fly off as four individual tracers while the hub returns with the drone.
  • Reference. Velocity and temperature profiles from the meteorological masts set the stability of the boundary layer for each launch, and the onboard sensors are cross-validated against sonic anemometers and mast sensors.

How a flight works

What happens on each flight, from release to the information the system provides.

01

Heated-wire release

Below the drone hangs a half-dome hub with four carbon-fibre rods, and a balloon with its own radiosonde is held at each rod tip. The drone climbs to release height upwind of the turbines, where heated wires inside the hub burn through all four strings at once. The four balloons drift off downwind as individual tracers; the hub stays with the drone.

02

Drift & measure

Each neutrally buoyant balloon follows the air through and around the turbine wakes, while its radiosonde samples position, pressure, temperature and humidity along the way.

03

Telemetry

The radiosonde streams its measurements by radio to a receiver on the ground during the flight, so nothing has to be recovered.

04

Tracks to profiles

Each GPS track gives the air's velocity along its path. Together with temperature and pressure, the system provides wind and potential-temperature profiles, and from those the stability of the boundary layer.

Results

Across three launches, the tracers passed through the turbines near the launch site and then through or around a second wind farm downstream, covering about 30 km, as the flight explorer above shows. The tracks and the pressure, altitude and humidity profiles along them show that the method can resolve mixing over large distances with useful spatial and temporal resolution. Next we will compare the field data with large eddy simulations from the Johns Hopkins Turbulence Database.
